Tayo is a CTF enthusiast and security learner who publishes detailed writeups for TryHackMe rooms and other Capture the Flag challenges. These writeups began as a personal reference and a way to sharpen the ability to explain technical concepts clearly, but they have grown into something genuinely useful for other learners on the same path. The focus is on understanding *why* techniques work, not just running commands — so every writeup includes explanations of the underlying concepts alongside the practical steps.

## What Are CTF Writeups?

A CTF writeup is a documented walkthrough of a Capture the Flag challenge — a controlled, intentionally vulnerable environment designed for learning offensive security techniques. Writeups record the commands run, the reasoning behind each decision, and the vulnerabilities exploited at each stage. They serve as study material, a way to revisit solved challenges, and a resource for anyone who gets stuck and needs a nudge in the right direction.

To get the most out of these writeups, work through the challenge yourself first. Use the writeup to check your approach, understand where you went wrong, or fill in gaps in your knowledge — not as a shortcut around the problem-solving process. The learning happens in the struggle.

## The Byte Lotus Series

Byte Lotus is a TryHackMe room that forms the core of the writeups on this site. It's a multi-stage challenge designed to test your skills across the full attack chain — from initial reconnaissance and service enumeration through to exploitation and privilege escalation. The room is well-suited for intermediate players looking to build confidence with real techniques in a structured environment. The writeups here cover every phase in depth, with enough context to make each step educational rather than just a sequence of commands to copy.

<Warning>
  These writeups are published strictly for educational purposes. Every technique documented here was performed against intentionally vulnerable machines on platforms like TryHackMe — environments you are explicitly authorized to attack. Always obtain proper authorization before testing any system. Never attempt these techniques against systems you do not have explicit permission to test. Only hack what you own or have written authorization to access.
</Warning>
